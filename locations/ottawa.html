Ottawa's Local HVAC Contractor — Serving Every Neighbourhood

Ottawa is a city of remarkable architectural diversity. The century-old brick semis and detached homes of the Glebe, Centretown, and Hintonburg sit alongside postwar bungalows in Alta Vista and Elmvale Acres, mid-century apartment buildings along Somerset and Rideau Streets, and the newer infill townhomes and low-rise condos that have appeared throughout inner Ottawa over the past two decades. That variety creates an equally diverse set of HVAC challenges — and it's exactly the kind of work Nepean Home Services has been doing across Ottawa for over 15 years.

Our TSSA-licensed gas fitters and certified refrigeration mechanics approach every Ottawa job with an understanding that no two homes are identical. A furnace replacement in a 1920s Westboro semi requires a fundamentally different assessment than a system upgrade in a 1990s Alta Vista two-storey — different ductwork configurations, different combustion air requirements, different venting options, and different structural constraints around equipment placement. We account for all of it before quoting, which is why our flat-rate estimates hold firm through installation day.

HVAC in Ottawa's Older Neighbourhoods

Central Ottawa's mature neighbourhoods — the Glebe, Old Ottawa South, Hintonburg, Mechanicsville, Vanier — present some of the most technically interesting HVAC work in the region. Many homes in these areas were built before forced-air heating was standard, and have been retrofit with systems that don't always integrate cleanly with the original architecture. Older gravity furnaces were replaced with forced-air in the 1970s and 80s, and those systems are now at or well past the end of their reliable service life. We specialize in navigating the constraints of older Ottawa homes — tight mechanical rooms, knob-and-tube wiring considerations, and limited exterior wall access for modern venting configurations.

Furnace Replacement Across Ottawa

Ottawa's climate demands a reliable furnace. With design heating temperatures reaching -24°C and real cold snaps pushing well below that, a failing or inefficient furnace isn't just uncomfortable — it's a genuine household risk. When Ottawa homeowners call us for a furnace replacement, we conduct a full Manual J heat load calculation specific to their home's square footage, insulation, window area, and orientation. That ensures the replacement unit is correctly sized for actual conditions — not just matched to what came out. Upgrading from a mid-efficiency 80% AFUE furnace to a modern 96% AFUE condensing unit typically reduces gas consumption by 25–35% annually.

Air Conditioning & Heat Pump Installation in Ottawa

Ottawa summers bring sustained heat and high humidity, and the right air conditioner size determines whether a home genuinely dehumidifies or simply cools without removing moisture. We calculate cooling loads from first principles — not rules of thumb — and select equipment that will operate efficiently across Ottawa's range of summer conditions. For homeowners interested in reducing natural gas use, cold-climate heat pumps are an increasingly practical option for Ottawa, capable of efficient operation through our full winter range when integrated with a gas backup. We handle heat pump installations city-wide, including the electrical coordination required for most residential systems.

Water Heater Service Across Ottawa

Ottawa's older inner-city homes frequently have water heaters tucked into tight utility spaces that create complications for replacement — low clearances, outdated venting configurations, and in some cases gas lines that need rerouting before a new unit can be safely installed. We've handled all of these situations and build the assessment of those constraints into our upfront quote. For homes ready to upgrade to on-demand hot water, Navien and Rinnai tankless systems are available with same-week installation. For straightforward tank replacements, we maintain stock of the most common sizes for same or next-day service across Ottawa.
